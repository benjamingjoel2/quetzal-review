The forest is still loud when you leave Flores. Mist lies in the low places along the road, and the light comes up green through the trees. A few days ago you stood on a temple at Tikal and looked out over a canopy that seemed to have no edge. Now the road runs east towards the place where that canopy is cut by a line on a map. On the far side the signs change language, the money changes, and by tonight you will be eating supper beside a river in Belize.

Guatemala and Belize are usually sold as separate trips. They do not have to be. The Petén, Guatemala's great northern lowland, and the Cayo District of western Belize are neighbours, sharing the same forest and the same deep Maya past. Put them together and you get the highlands and Tikal on one side, river country and the Caribbean reef on the other, joined by a single land crossing.

## Why the combination works

Many travellers who visit Tikal fly to Flores, see the ruins and fly back to Guatemala City. But from the Petén, Belize is close by road. That turns a there-and-back into a line: Tikal, then the border, then San Ignacio and the Cayo District, then the coast and the cayes. You never retrace your steps, and the last part of the trip is the most restful one, on the water.

In Cayo you are still in Maya country. Xunantunich is not far from the border and makes an easy first stop after the crossing. Caracol is much further into the hills, a long drive on rough roads, and deserves a full day of its own. Between them are river lodges, caves and waterfalls, and the market town of San Ignacio.

Then you go east to the sea. Belize's cayes, small islands strung along the inside of the barrier reef, are where the trip slows right down: sand streets, wooden docks, and snorkelling a short boat ride away.

## The land border: Melchor de Mencos and Benque Viejo del Carmen

The crossing between the Petén and western Belize is at Melchor de Mencos on the Guatemalan side and Benque Viejo del Carmen on the Belizean side. You leave one country's immigration post and walk or drive across to the other. Allow time for two sets of formalities, and cross in daylight.

Entry and exit rules depend on your nationality and change from time to time. We have deliberately not listed fees or amounts here, because they change and because the only figure that matters is the one in force on the day you cross. Check these official pages before you go:

- **Leaving Guatemala.** The UK FCDO says that before you enter or exit Guatemala you must complete the online immigration declaration form, and that you should make sure your passport is stamped on entry ([UK FCDO, Guatemala entry requirements](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/guatemala/entry-requirements), checked November 2026). The same page warns that officials at land borders sometimes ask travellers for an "entry fee", that this is illegal, and that you should ask for an official receipt. The Guatemalan migration authority is the [Instituto Guatemalteco de Migración](https://igm.gob.gt/) (in Spanish).
- **Entering Belize.** For British passport holders, the FCDO says your passport must have an expiry date more than three months after the date you arrive, and that you can visit Belize without a visa for 30 days ([UK FCDO, Belize entry requirements](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/belize/entry-requirements), checked November 2026). Other nationalities should check the country list on the [Belize Immigration and Nationality Department's visa page](https://immigration.gov.bz/visa/visa-who-qualify/), checked November 2026.
- **Leaving Belize by land.** The FCDO says a departure tax must be paid at the border if you leave Belize by land or water to Guatemala or Mexico. Check the current amount on the [FCDO Belize entry requirements page](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/belize/entry-requirements) before you travel.
- **Coming back into Guatemala.** If your route loops back, remember the FCDO's guidance that Guatemala requires a passport with an expiry date at least six months after the date you arrive and at least two blank pages ([UK FCDO, Guatemala entry requirements](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/guatemala/entry-requirements), checked November 2026).

US citizens should also read the State Department's country information and entry pages for both Guatemala and Belize on travel.state.gov. Whatever passport you hold, re-check every page above in the week before you travel.

## Safety near the border

This is a well-used crossing, but it sits on a sensitive frontier, and current advice is worth reading in full rather than in summary. As of November 2026, the UK FCDO says:

- On the Guatemalan side, it advises against all but essential travel to parts of Guatemala, but those areas are along the Mexican border and in specific towns in Huehuetenango, not the Belize border. It does say to "take care near the Belize-Guatemala border because of the ongoing dispute between the 2 countries" and to "use only officially recognised border crossings" ([UK FCDO, Guatemala regional risks](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/guatemala/regional-risks), checked November 2026). Its general advice for Guatemala includes not travelling alone or at night, especially near borders.
- On the Belizean side, it says armed criminals have been active in the remote forested areas of Belize, particularly the border area with Guatemala and close to tourist sites there, but that the area is patrolled by the Belize Defence Force and there have been no reported incidents involving tourists ([UK FCDO, Belize safety and security](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/belize/safety-and-security), checked November 2026). It also recommends using a qualified guide for trips off the beaten track.
- The FCDO reports that the Government of Belize announced a 30-day State of Emergency on 1 November in parts of Belize City and the Belize and Cayo Districts, following an increase in gang-related violence. In Cayo, the listed areas are Cotton Tree Village and Unitedville Village, including streets and residential areas running parallel to the George Price Highway. The FCDO advises carrying valid ID, remaining vigilant and following the instructions of local authorities, and says there may be more police and checkpoints ([UK FCDO, Belize travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/belize), checked November 2026). The George Price Highway is the main road east from the border, so check whether this is still in force when you travel.

The US State Department's travel advisories, as listed in its official advisory feed, currently rate Guatemala "Level 3: Reconsider Travel", due to crime and terrorism, with some areas at higher risk, and Belize "Level 2: Exercise Increased Caution", due to crime ([US State Department travel advisories](https://travel.state.gov/_res/rss/TAsTWs.xml), checked November 2026). For Belize, it says US government employees are strongly discouraged from driving between cities after dark, and that US citizens should follow the same guidance.

In practice this means using the official crossing, travelling by day, arranging transfers in advance rather than improvising on the spot, and going to more remote sites such as Caracol with a guide. Our guide to [whether Guatemala is safe for travellers](/posts/is-guatemala-safe-for-travellers/) covers the Guatemalan side more broadly. Advice changes, so read both countries' pages again just before you go.

## A suggested route shape

There is no single right way to do this, but around twelve to fourteen days lets you see both countries without rushing. As a starting point:

- **Days 1 to 3: Antigua.** Arrive in Guatemala City and settle into Antigua, with time to walk the old town and recover from the flight.
- **Days 4 to 6: the highlands and Lake Atitlán.** Chichicastenango on a market day, then the lake. Our [10-day Guatemala itinerary](/posts/ten-day-guatemala-itinerary/) has the detail.
- **Days 7 to 8: Flores and Tikal.** Fly north from Guatemala City and give Tikal an early start. Our guide to [visiting Tikal at sunrise](/posts/visiting-tikal-at-sunrise/) explains how.
- **Day 9: cross into Belize.** A morning crossing at Melchor de Mencos, Xunantunich in the afternoon, and the evening in Cayo.
- **Days 10 to 11: Cayo.** A river day, a cave or the pine forest, and a long day for Caracol if ruins are your thing.
- **Days 12 to 14: the cayes.** Head east to the coast and out to the cayes to snorkel, swim and do very little, then fly home from Belize.

If you have less time, drop the highlands and start in the Petén. If you have more, add nights on the reef, where a spare day is never wasted. Check flight options carefully, since flying into Guatemala and out of Belize is usually the neatest arrangement.

## The Belize Barrier Reef

The reef is the reason to finish in Belize. The Belize Barrier Reef Reserve System was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1996, and UNESCO describes the coastal area of Belize as an outstanding natural system consisting of the largest barrier reef in the northern hemisphere, offshore atolls, several hundred sand cays, mangrove forests, coastal lagoons and estuaries, with seven sites that are a significant habitat for threatened species including marine turtles, manatees and the American marine crocodile ([UNESCO World Heritage Centre](https://whc.unesco.org/en/news/1764), checked November 2026).

What that means on the water is coral gardens close to the cayes, and further out the atolls for those willing to take a longer boat ride. Choose a reef-safe sunscreen, do not touch or stand on coral, and go with licensed guides who keep to the rules of the marine reserves.

On timing, the FCDO says Belize's hurricane season runs from 1 June to 30 November ([UK FCDO, Belize safety and security](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/belize/safety-and-security), checked November 2026), which overlaps with Guatemala's rainy season. Travelling in those months is possible, but leave slack in the plan and keep an eye on forecasts.
